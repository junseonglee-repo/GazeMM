C. Training settings

Architecture and objective

An ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 produces a 512-dimensional embedding, $L_2$-normalized before matching. Input NIR periocular images are padded to a square with the aspect ratio preserved, resized to 224×224 and replicated across three channels. No iris segmentation or polar normalization is applied. The network is optimized with the soft-margin batch-hard triplet loss:

$$\ell = \mathrm{softplus}\big(d(a,p) - d(a,n)\big) = \log\big(1 + e^{\,d(a,p) - d(a,n)}\big)$$

Evaluation protocol

Subject-disjoint 2-fold cross-validation with three fixed random seeds. GazeMM, UE-UGCL and Maxout-BA are trained on each training fold, with 15% of the training identities held out for validation; the checkpoint with the lowest validation EER is selected for testing. All remaining methods are evaluated on the same test folds. GazeMM uses leave-one-out identity-specific affine subspaces for matching, while comparison methods follow their original matching protocols. Quality-filter statistics are computed once per dataset over all images.

D. Hyperparameter analyses

Subspace dimension $k$

$k$ controls how much intra-class variation the affine subspace is allowed to absorb. Too small and gaze-induced variation still leaks into the residual; too large and the subspace starts absorbing impostor directions as well, eroding the genuine/impostor margin. The effective dimension is capped at $\min(k,\,N-1,\,D)$.

Quality filter strictness $c$

The Laplacian-variance threshold is set in the log domain from the median and MAD, so $c$ scales how many standard-deviation-equivalents below the median a frame may fall before rejection. Larger $c$ keeps more frames; smaller $c$ trades recall for input quality and raises FTE.

$$\text{keep} \iff \log f \;\ge\; \mathrm{median}(\log f) - c \cdot 1.4826 \cdot \mathrm{MAD}(\log f)$$
